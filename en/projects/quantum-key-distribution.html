Quantum Key Distribution System with Real-Time Eavesdropping Detection

In a Raspberry Pi based system developed with my teammates, we modeled the BB84 and B92 quantum key distribution protocols, detected possible eavesdropping attempts in real time via QBER, and used the secure keys for AES-GCM encrypted data transmission.

Problem and approach

This work is a prototype and test environment designed to study QKD protocols on a low-cost embedded system. It is not an operational quantum communication link using a physical single-photon source or photon detector. The BB84 and B92 processes, including bit generation, basis selection, measurement, sifting, and eavesdropper scenarios, were modeled algorithmically on a Raspberry Pi.

Measured results

BB84 shared bits
20.45 on average
B92: 10.12 on average
10% QBER threshold
98% blocking rate
2% false alarms
Eavesdropping scenario
BB84 QBER: 26.3%
B92 QBER: 32.1%

Under normal transmission, the average QBER was measured at 2.1% for BB84 and 3.4% for B92. In eavesdropping scenarios, QBER values rose above 20%; the 10% threshold therefore offered the best balance between detection and false alarm rate.

These numbers also match the theory. In BB84, Alice’s and Bob’s bases match half the time on average; in B92, only a quarter of the time. That is why BB84 produced about twice as many shared bits (20.45 vs. 10.12). In an attack where the eavesdropper measures and resends every photon, the expected QBER for BB84 is 25%; the 26.3% we measured is very close to it.

Technical flow

  1. 1. Protocol modeling

    We modeled the BB84 and B92 protocols in Python on a Raspberry Pi 3 B+; in our tests, BB84 produced roughly twice as many shared bits.

  2. 2. QBER-based eavesdropping detection

    We monitored the ratio of erroneous bits to total transmitted bits. When QBER exceeded the 10% threshold, the channel was deemed insecure and key generation was stopped.

  3. 3. Hybrid secure communication

    We built a hybrid structure by integrating the secure keys that stayed below the QBER threshold into AES-GCM encrypted data transmission.

What I gained from this project

This work gave me the opportunity to approach embedded system development and quantum communication concepts together. Presenting a technical system at a congress allowed me to connect with experts in defense technologies, cybersecurity, and quantum technologies, and to deepen my knowledge.
