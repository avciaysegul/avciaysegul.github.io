SAVTEK 2026 · METU Culture and Convention Center

Quantum Key Distribution System with Real-Time Eavesdropping Detection

In a Raspberry Pi based system developed with my teammates, we modelled the BB84 and B92 quantum key distribution protocols, detected possible eavesdropping attempts in real time via QBER, and used the secure keys for AES-GCM encrypted data transmission.

Presentation
SAVTEK 2026
9–11 September 2026
Venue
METU Culture and Convention Center
Ankara
Role
Team member · development and technical presentation

(01)

Problem and approach

This work is a prototype and test environment designed to study QKD protocols on a low-cost embedded system. It is not an operational quantum communication link using a physical single-photon source or photon detector. The BB84 and B92 processes, including bit generation, basis selection, measurement, sifting, and eavesdropper scenarios, were modelled algorithmically on a Raspberry Pi.

  • Raspberry Pi 3 B+
  • Raspberry Pi OS
  • Python
  • BB84
  • B92
  • QBER
  • AES-GCM

(02)

Measured results

BB84 shared bits
20.45 on average
B92: 10.12 on average
10% QBER threshold
98% blocking rate
2% false alarms
Eavesdropping scenario
BB84 QBER: 26.3%
B92 QBER: 32.1%

Under normal transmission, the average QBER was measured at 2.1% for BB84 and 3.4% for B92. In eavesdropping scenarios, QBER values rose above 20%; the 10% threshold therefore offered the best balance between detection and false alarm rate.

(03)

Technical flow

  1. 01

    1. Protocol modelling

    We modelled the BB84 and B92 protocols in Python on a Raspberry Pi 3 B+; in our tests, BB84 produced roughly twice as many shared bits.

  2. 02

    2. QBER-based eavesdropping detection

    We monitored the ratio of erroneous bits to total transmitted bits. When QBER exceeded the 10% threshold, the channel was deemed insecure and key generation was stopped.

  3. 03

    3. Hybrid secure communication

    We built a hybrid structure by integrating the secure keys that stayed below the QBER threshold into AES-GCM encrypted data transmission.

What I gained from this project

This work gave me the opportunity to approach embedded system development and quantum communication concepts together. Presenting a technical system at a congress allowed me to connect with experts in defence technologies, cybersecurity, and quantum technologies, and to deepen my knowledge.
